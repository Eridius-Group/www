On-Site Server Hardware

At the heart of our operations is "Root," our custom on-site server infrastructure powered by a Kubernetes cluster of Single Board Computers (SBCs). Our current setup features Orange Pi 6 Plus nodes, each equipped with 32GB of RAM. These highly efficient nodes draw roughly 30 watts at baseline and peak at 60 watts under heavy load (source). To expand our capabilities, we are integrating Radxa Orion boards and Radxa Fogwise AIrbox Q900 units, both of which operate within an energy-efficient 65-watt limit (Radxa AIrbox Q900 specs) and (Radxa Orion O6N specs).

For our AI-based workloads—specifically targeted tasks like code auditing, content classification, and practical quality-of-life tools—we utilize the Radxa Fogwise AIrbox Q900, which is purpose-built for specialized AI processes. We are committed to focused and sustainable computing, and we do not intend to train Large Language Models (LLMs) or generative AI on our Root infrastructure.

All other workloads are seamlessly balanced across our Radxa Orion O6N and Orange Pi 6 Plus boards. To ensure fast data access and redundancy, these nodes are equipped with dual Gen4 NVMe SSDs configured in a RAID array. We also prioritize absolute uptime; in the event of a primary network outage, a dedicated Radxa Orion O6N equipped with a 5G network card provides automatic failsafe internet access.

Our network backbone is just as robust. We rely on the Palmshell NeXT H2 to manage our dual 5G and fiber-optic connections, serving simultaneously as our firewall, router, and core security appliance. Everything is tied together using an HP network switch equipped with QSFP+ ports. We currently use adapters to step down to RJ45, ensuring our network is completely future-proofed and ready for native QSFP+ expansion when the time comes.

Server Software

To power our infrastructure, we developed CoreK30s - a custom, open-source Linux distribution tailored specifically for Kubernetes. Designed with a minimal footprint similar to Alpine Linux, it applies hardened-linux patches to the latest kernel and relies only on essential modules to operate securely.

  • FIPS-Ready Architecture: Compiled to meet FIPS compliance standards out of the box, laying the groundwork for future official certification.
  • Strict Access Controls: Fortified with SELinux in enforcing mode, restricted root-level permissions, and custom, limited-scope commands that replace standard sudo.
  • Immutable Infrastructure: Employs read-only filesystem mounts to thwart unauthorized modifications. Crucial settings (like LDAP syncing, S3 credentials, and SSH requirements), the bootloader, and kernel modules are permanently locked post-configuration.
  • Secure Boot & Encryption: Unlocking the encrypted disks during boot requires a cryptographic handshake using both a TPM and a network confirmation server.
  • Centralized Authentication: Users are managed with LDAP via PAM, ensuring all nodes perfectly sync active usernames, SSH keys, and hardware TOTP requirements.
  • Automated Auditing: Built-in audit logging tools feature a daemon that automatically uploads system logs to S3 storage daily at midnight UTC.
  • Seamless Updates: System updates follow a resilient A/B partition pattern, allowing for frequent live patching via remounts without disrupting operations.
  • Flexible Build Process: Engineered to be compiled from scratch for ultimate security. We also offer pre-compiled drop-in sources to speed up the build process, though the final ISO still requires node-specific configuration.

We are actively refining CoreK30s and plan to release the full source code publicly in the near future.
